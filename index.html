Codebook

Political Influence

Events protesting perceived foreign interference in domestic political processes — influence operations, disinformation, media manipulation, backing of political parties, or pressure to shape government policy.

Typical events: protests against foreign-backed candidates; demonstrations against foreign-funded NGOs; incidents targeting political lobbying.

Project Grievance

Protests, strikes, or violence directed at a specific foreign-funded or foreign-operated project — mine, dam, road, or construction site — over local conditions: wages, labour practices, environmental damage, or safety.

Boundary: grievance targets a specific site's conduct, not foreign presence in the country generally (see Sovereignty & Territorial).

Sovereignty & Territorial

Contestation of a foreign power's presence at a national or strategic level — opposition to military bases, troop deployments, territorial claims, or security arrangements seen as compromising sovereignty.

Boundary: the demand is for national-level withdrawal or territorial concession, not reform of a specific site (see Project Grievance).

Military Forces Attacks

Armed kinetic engagements against the target country's military forces and assets — bases, warships, aircraft, convoys, personnel. Distinguished from civilian protest about military presence (which falls under Sovereignty & Territorial).

Typical events: missile and drone strikes on warships; attacks on military bases; armed engagements with military personnel; IED and ambush attacks on convoys.

Diplomatic/Bilateral

Events targeting the bilateral relationship itself — embassy and consulate protests, demonstrations during state visits, responses to specific diplomatic incidents such as expulsions or sanctions.

Economic Competition

Grievances about foreign economic dominance, trade practices, or market competition at a policy or sector level — broader than Project Grievance. Includes protests against foreign goods undercutting local industries, economic dependency, or foreign investment policy.

Domestic (Solidarity)

Events expressing solidarity with politically repressed or marginalised groups within the target country — Tibet, Xinjiang/Uyghurs, Hong Kong democracy movements, and equivalent causes relating to US domestic politics and rights abuses.

Typical events: solidarity rallies at embassies or consulates; commemorations; international protests responding to the foreign power's domestic political actions.

Violence Against Nationals

Physical attacks, killings, kidnappings, or targeted violence against nationals, workers, military personnel, diplomatic facilities, or diaspora communities of the target country.

Typical events: armed attacks on foreign workers at project sites; mob violence targeting diaspora districts; kidnappings; attacks on military convoys or diplomatic premises.

Xenophobia/Anti-Immigration

Violence, discrimination, or exclusionary mobilisation targeting nationals or migrants of the target country as an ethnic or cultural group — driven by hostility toward the people rather than opposition to state policy.

Typical events: hate crimes against diaspora communities; exclusionary campaigns against foreign traders; ethnically targeted violence.

Symbolic/Nationalist

Demonstrations expressing general nationalist opposition to a foreign power without a specific policy grievance — tied to historical anniversaries, national holidays, or broad anti-foreign sentiment. The residual category for expressive rather than instrumental activity.

The Typology scheme groups events by the grievance dimension driving them. Its last three categories cover events not substantively about China or the US; these are flagged rather than dropped, and can be hidden via Response & fatalities → Hide flagged events.

Sovereignty-Security

Foreign military presence, armed operations, and territorial claims — the hard-security face of sovereignty.

Detail: Codes: S-MI-civ (military presence, civil), S-MI-arm (military presence, armed), S-TE (territorial). Largest category; mostly events Breakdown files under Military Forces Attacks or Sovereignty & Territorial.

Sovereignty-Identity

Objections to interference in domestic politics — official visits, diplomatic presence, and perceived meddling in internal affairs.

Detail: Codes: S-DI, S-PI. Overwhelmingly coded “sovereignty (perceived)” rather than direct territorial infringement.

Power-Political

Domestic regime contests in which the target power is invoked by one side — the grievance is about who governs, with China or the US implicated in that argument.

Detail: Code: P-RO (regime opposition).

Power-Proxy

Events where the target power is engaged at one remove — through a partner state, an allied force, or a third-party project rather than directly.

Detail: Small category (49). Mixed codes; often Baloch, Sahel, or partner-state contexts.

Value-Identity

Solidarity or hostility grounded in shared identity — ethnic, national, religious, or diasporic kinship with a group associated with the target power.

Detail: Codes: V-IDd / V-ID (co-identity). Note the project’s identity-robustness variant reassigns ~1,083 co-identity rows from Values to Sovereignty.

Value-Ideology

Normative and ideological claims — human rights, democracy, and political-solidarity campaigns directed at or about the target power.

Detail: Codes: V-SO (solidarity), V-HR (human rights), V-HE, V-SOC.

Material-Local

Concrete local grievances at a specific site or project — wages and labour conditions, resource extraction, environmental harm, land.

Detail: Codes: E-LA (labour), E-RE (resources), E-EN, E-IM. Corresponds closely to Breakdown’s Project Grievance.

Material-Nationalism

Economic nationalism — tariffs, trade competition, and protection of domestic industry framed against the target power.

Detail: Codes: S-TR (trade-as-sovereignty), E-TR (trade).

Immigration/Xenophobia

Hostility toward migrants, workers, or residents associated with the target power, expressed as anti-immigration or xenophobic action.

Detail: Codes: I-XN, I-RH, I-SC. Split out of Material-Local, where the source scheme folds it in.

Attacks on nationals

Violence against Chinese or US nationals with no established political motive — robbery, kidnapping, banditry.

Detail: Absorbs the source scheme’s Criminal class (INVALID-CRIMINAL); 96% of it is coded Violence Against Nationals under Breakdown. Distinguished from Unclear by the motive being positively assessed as non-political.

Incidental

The link to China or the US is incidental — the event is not substantively about either power.

Detail: INVALID-INCIDENTAL. Mostly sourcing-stage keyword false positives, e.g. protests matched only because they blocked the “Pan-American” or “Inter-American” highway, or were held at a venue with “American” in its name.

Unclear

A national was harmed, but sources cannot establish whether nationality was the reason — the honest “can’t tell” bucket between anti-power activity and ordinary crime.

Detail: INVALID-UNCLEAR. 78% coded Violence Against Nationals; concentrated in conflict states (Sudan, Myanmar, Nigeria, CAR, Iraq, Pakistan, DRC) and rising sharply — 2 events in the 1990s to 62 in the 2020s.
